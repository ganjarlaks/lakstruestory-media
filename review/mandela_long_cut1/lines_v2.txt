H1 | Twenty seven years in prison. Then one walk out.
H2 | On 11 February 1990, Nelson Mandela walked out of Victor Verster Prison, near Paarl, in South Africa.
H3 | This is the story of what put him there, what he refused, and what he said when he got out.
1.1 | On 21 March 1960, police killed sixty nine peaceful protesters at Sharpeville, in South Africa.
1.2 | Eighteen days later, the government banned the African National Congress, the ANC, which Mandela had joined in 1944.
1.3 | Mandela had been on trial for treason since 1956. In March 1961, he was acquitted, and he went underground.
1.4 | In hiding, he became known as the Black Pimpernel.
1.5 | He had argued for an armed wing of the ANC, and it was formed, under the name Umkhonto we Sizwe, Spear of the Nation.
1.6 | In 1993, the Nobel Committee said that he had first been inspired by Gandhi's methods of nonviolence, but that the climate hardened after Sharpeville.
1.7 | In 1961, a film crew interviewed him, and the year is burned into the picture. Listen to what he says about peace.
1.8 | In January 1962, he left the country for military training. In July he came back, and on 5 August, he was arrested at a roadblock near Howick.
1.9 | In November that year, he was sentenced to five years, for incitement and for leaving the country illegally.
1.10 | Then came a much bigger case. In October 1963, he stood in the Palace of Justice in Pretoria with other accused, and the state alleged 199 acts of sabotage.
1.11 | The first indictment was thrown out, and the accused were arrested again at once.
1.12 | On 20 April 1964, instead of giving evidence, he made a long statement from the dock.
1.13 | He said that he had fought against white domination, and that he had fought against black domination.
1.14 | He ended by saying that freedom was an ideal he hoped to live for and to achieve, but that if needs be, it was an ideal for which he was prepared to die.
1.15 | On 11 June 1964, the court convicted all but one of the accused. The next day, Mandela and seven others were sentenced to life in prison.
1.16 | He was sent to Robben Island, and became prisoner number 466 of 1964.
2.1 | In 1968, his mother died. In 1969, his eldest son died in a car accident. He was forbidden to go to either funeral.
2.2 | In 1993, on American television, he spoke about what it had cost his family. Here is part of that interview.
2.3 | In 1982, after nearly eighteen years on Robben Island, he was moved to Pollsmoor Prison, as prisoner number 220 of 1982.
2.4 | The Nobel Foundation's own biography says that his reputation grew steadily in prison, and that he consistently refused to compromise his political position to obtain his freedom.
2.5 | In February 1985, President P. W. Botha offered to free him and other political prisoners, if they renounced violence.
2.6 | Mandela refused. His answer was read out at a rally in Soweto by his daughter. Here it is.
2.7 | Later that year, after surgery, he was held apart from his comrades, and began communicating with the government about possible talks with the African National Congress.
2.8 | In 1986, he had his first meeting with the Justice Minister, Kobie Coetsee. In 1988, he was treated for tuberculosis, and in December he was moved to his last prison, Victor Verster, as prisoner number 1335 of 1988.
2.9 | In July 1989, he met P. W. Botha, the president who had offered him conditional freedom, in his Cape Town office. In October, his old comrade Walter Sisulu and four other prisoners were released.
2.10 | In December 1989, he met the new president, F. W. de Klerk. On 2 February 1990, de Klerk told Parliament that the African National Congress and all other political organisations were no longer banned.
2.11 | Nine days later, Mandela would walk free.
3.1 | In the days before the release, Mandela met de Klerk and was told the date.
3.2 | He objected to a plan to release him in Johannesburg. He wanted to walk out of Victor Verster.
3.3 | He also asked for more time, so his supporters could prepare. De Klerk refused the delay, but agreed to the prison gate.
3.4 | On 10 February, the president told the press the date, the place and the hour.
3.5 | Here is that announcement.
3.6 | He was due out at about three o'clock, but he did not walk free until after four.
3.7 | He was seventy one years old, and few people had seen a recent picture of him.
3.8 | He walked out beside his wife Winnie, and the two raised their fists.
3.9 | His car was then trapped in the crowds for about an hour.
3.10 | He reached Cape Town City Hall as night fell, and spoke to the crowd from the balcony.
3.11 | He read his speech wearing borrowed reading glasses.
3.12 | That night, he slept at Bishopscourt, the home of Archbishop Desmond Tutu.
3.13 | Next day, a Cape Town newspaper carried the headline free at last.
4.1 | The next morning, 12 February, he held his first international news conference, with Winnie at his side.
4.2 | That day he flew to Johannesburg. The next, 13 February, he spoke at a welcome rally at the stadium in Soweto, and slept that night in his family home in Orlando West, for the first time in decades.
4.3 | He said he was overwhelmed by the welcome.
4.4 | Asked about peace and the armed struggle, he said there was no conflict between the two.
4.5 | In 1991, the ANC held its first national conference inside South Africa since the ban of 1960, and elected him its president.
4.6 | The Nobel Committee said later that he went straight from prison to the negotiating table.
4.7 | In September 1992, he and de Klerk signed a Record of Understanding on how a new constitution would be written. In June 1993, the date of the first free election was set.
4.8 | It was not peaceful. In 1993, the Nobel Committee said that the number of people killed during the negotiations was in the tens of thousands.
4.9 | In the 1993 American interview, he also spoke about President de Klerk.
4.10 | In 1993, the Nobel Peace Prize went to Mandela and to de Klerk, the president who had released him, for their work to end apartheid peacefully.
4.11 | He accepted it in Oslo, on 10 December 1993, and paid tribute to de Klerk.
4.12 | In April 1994, South Africa held its first election open to every race, and about twenty two million people voted.
4.13 | Mandela and the African National Congress won.
4.14 | On 10 May 1994, he was sworn in as South Africa's first Black president.
4.15 | History.com reports that in his inaugural address he said the time for the healing of wounds had come.
4.16 | In 1994, a Swedish journalist interviewed him in the presidential palace in Pretoria. Here is part of it.
4.17 | As president, he set up a Truth and Reconciliation Commission to investigate human rights violations under apartheid, and in 1996 he presided over a new constitution.
4.18 | He retired in June 1999, at the age of eighty.
4.19 | Nelson Mandela died on 5 December 2013, aged ninety five.
