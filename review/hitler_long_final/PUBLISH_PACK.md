## Description (draft)
Hitler's Last Day: What Does the Record Say? On 30 April 1945 Adolf Hitler died in a bunker under Berlin. Within six weeks Moscow was saying he might still be alive. This film follows the last days from the Soviet attack on Berlin to the marriage and the testament, the day itself, and the aftermath, and says plainly what the records show and what they leave open.

Chapters
0:00 Cold open
0:19 Berlin, April 1945
1:48 The Last Days
3:19 30 April
4:34 What Was Left
8:01 The question
8:15 Research references

Nobody filmed the events of 29 and 30 April. Those chapters run over period footage and documents, labelled on screen; the closing section says which evidence is physical and which is not.

Sources: NARA Prologue, Spring 2015 (Hitler's will, marriage register, 1946 War Department letters); Beevor, Berlin 1945; Trevor-Roper, The Last Days of Hitler (1947); The Hitler Book (2005); Charlier et al. 2018 (teeth); phys.org, ABC News and UConn Today 2009 (skull fragment); Mutual War News 1 May 1945.
Footage (short excerpts, archive.org): US Army Signal Corps / NARA films; Universal Newsreel 'Hitler's Heyday' (1947), 'Germany Gives Up', 'Reds Roll On in Germany', 'Allies Sign Control Law for Germany', 'Allies Seize German Loot and Criminals'; Prelinger 'Postwar Germany' (1947).
Music: Brylie Christopher Oxley, solo guitar, CC0 (archive.org). No piano, no synthetic music.
Narration: Fraser (AI-generated voice). Subtitles on real archive speech are machine transcripts.
