The most famous story about chewing gum says it began with a failed rubber experiment and an exiled Mexican president. Parts of that story are legend, and this film says which. We follow chicle from the Maya forest, through a patent, a soap salesman and a world war, to the day a country banned the stuff.

Where the record is thin: the Santa Anna and Thomas Adams meeting is told with hedges because the sources disagree and none we found cites a record from the time. The Black Jack date (1884 or 1875) and the 1888 vending machines are also reported with hedges.

Footage disclosure
There is no film of chicle harvesting, gum factories or the people in this story that we could use. All footage is stand-in material and is labelled on screen at first use in each chapter. About 83% of the visible footage is colour (1953 US National Archives film on Middle America, 1967 US Army aerial views of Manhattan and the Hudson, 2018 El Mirador drone and ground footage, 2022 Wrigley Field drone footage) and about 17% is black and white (1897 to 1903 Library of Congress films and the 1944 Army K-ration film), kept only where the narration names those films. The 1953 film has a magenta cast from its print; this is neutralised, with no stylised grade. Colour footage has a natural grade; black-and-white clips carry a light neutral tone. Footage specific to gum: none. Narration is synthetic (Kokoro TTS).
