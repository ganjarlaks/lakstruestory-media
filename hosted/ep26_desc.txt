In 1806 a Boston businessman loaded a ship with ice cut from a frozen pond and sailed it to the Caribbean. Much of it melted. A century later the cold finally came out of a wall socket, thanks to a gas that turned out to hurt the sky. This is the story of the refrigerator: a merchant, a doctor, a brewer's engineer and the chemists who made the gas.

A note on the evidence: dates and names in this film were checked against second sources where we could find them, and hedged wording means the sources differ. Household refrigerator figures for 1930 and 1940 vary between sources, so we say "about one in ten" and "around half". Perkins is placed "in the mid-eighteen-thirties" (ASME gives an 1834 patent and an 1835 demonstration). The Domelre is dated "around 1913 or 1914" because sources disagree. The debtors' prison detail for Tudor rests on one account and is narrated as "by one account". We could not independently confirm the Wikipedia-only dates for Mellowes, Kelvinator and Frigidaire, so the film does not state them. Linde's 1873 and 1876 dates rest on a Wikipedia article and a brewing dictionary.

Footage disclosure
About 66% of this film is archival footage (including the chapter cards, which sit over footage) and about 32% is original hand-drawn explainer animation by Odd Making; the 10-second sources card is the rest. Strictly "moving pictures of the subject" (ice harvesting, kitchens, home refrigerators) is about 47%. The remainder of the archive share is diagram films and NASA animations used as stand-ins. No film here shows the events as they happened: the 1902 and 1919 ice harvests stand in for the 19th-century trade, and nothing in the archive shows a home refrigerator between 1913 and the 1927 advertisement.
